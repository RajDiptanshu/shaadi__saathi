# Mogra & Moti — creative concept

Phase 1, 2026-09-16. Branch `feature/mogra-moti`. A fictional demo: **Rohan Malhotra & Anaya Kapoor,
15 February 2027**. Every other invented detail is a flagged placeholder (see `scene-architecture.md` §7).

Companion documents: `visual-dna.md` (colour, photography, illustration, composition),
`typography-system.md`, `material-library.md`, `animation-storyboard.md` (motion, camera, transitions,
interaction, sound, storyboard), `asset-plan.md`, `scene-architecture.md`.

### Decisions this direction is built on (from the user, 2026-09-16)

- The legacy `site/demo/mogra-moti/` stays where it is, untouched. This is a separate new experience and
  inherits nothing from it visually.
- Reduced motion is a shorter, calmer cut of the *same* film: opening narrative and major transitions stay;
  camera push, parallax, scroll-linked and large spatial movement go; continuous motion is reduced.
- Photography is editorial by default (hands, mehendi, jewellery, fabric, flowers, stationery, architecture,
  venues, silhouettes, people from behind, cropped portraits, atmosphere). Faces are allowed when the
  photograph is genuinely good and properly licensed. Authentic quality beats avoiding faces.
- No premium stock purchase yet; this phase defines exactly what imagery is needed.
- Fictional details are sensible placeholders held in one data file, never in components.

## The idea

### मोती पिरोना — *moti pirona*, to string pearls

In Hindi, to string pearls is also what you say of anything made beautifully, one piece at a time.

On a wedding morning, mogra is bought as tight green-white buds and strung by hand into garlands. The buds
are closed when they're threaded. **They open at night**, and that is when the fragrance comes.

A wedding is the same shape: two separate lives threaded onto one strand, with care, in daylight — and the
thing it was all for arrives after dark.

> **Mogra & Moti is one strand being made, from dawn to night: bud by bud, pearl by pearl, until the knot is
> tied and every flower has opened.**

---

## 1. Emotional concept

**The feeling: anticipation that ripens.** Not celebration yet — the quiet, tender hours before it, when
people's hands are busy making something for someone they love.

| The guest should feel | Created by | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Hushed curiosity in the first seconds | silence, low light slowly growing, one object moving | a loud reveal, a bang of gold |
| That this was made by hand, for them | tactile paper, thread, real flowers, precise small movements | anything that looks generated or templated |
| Welcomed, not sold to | restraint, few words, space | banners, badges, pills, exclamation marks |
| The day warming as they scroll | light temperature and bloom state changing with each chapter | the same flat ivory from top to bottom |
| A resolution at the end | the knot tied, the last bud open, the music settling | "Thank you ❤️" in a footer |

**Why this emotion.** Every competitor sells the *reveal*: a curtain, an envelope, a door, a scratch card.
Their emotion peaks in the first two seconds and flattens afterwards (the mallikainvitestudio teardown:
"everything is pale, so nothing builds"). An arc of anticipation → warmth → bloom gives the scroll a reason
to exist, and puts the emotional peak at the *end*, where the RSVP and the memory are.

## 2. Visual narrative

The invitation is a single day in light, and a single strand in objects.

| Chapter | Scene | Light | Mogra | The strand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dawn | 01 Opening | cool, low, raking from top-left | tight closed buds | a pearl arrives; the thread goes taut; beads are strung |
| Morning | 02 Welcome | soft window light | buds, cool white | the thread carries into their names |
| Midday | 03 Our Story | clear, even, brightest | buds loosening | invisible — the story is theirs |
| Afternoon → night | 04 Celebrations | warming event by event | half-open | the thread draws each celebration in; at the Wedding it becomes the knot |
| Dusk | 05 Venue | gold, horizontal | opening | — |
| Night | 06 Gallery | lamp light on dark | open | — |
| Night | 07 RSVP | lamp light | open | a guest's reply adds a pearl to the strand |
| Late night | 08 Closing | one warm lamp in darkness | full bloom | the two ends are knotted; the strand rests |

**Why a time-of-day arc.** It is true to the flower (buds close in the morning, bloom and scent at night),
true to the wedding (Mehendi in the afternoon, pheras at night), and it gives the page a colour build from
ivory to warm dark that no pale template has. The arc is *felt* through light, grading and flower state —
it is never labelled.

**Why a strand.** Threading is the act that joins *mogra* and *moti*; it is the gajra, the veni, the pearl
satlada, the mangalsutra (literally "auspicious thread") and the gathbandhan knot tied during the pheras.
It carries Indian meaning through a gesture, not through ornament.

## 3. What the market does, and where we differ

From `research/launch-brief.html` and `research/reference-teardown.md`:

| Market convention | Seen at | Mogra & Moti instead |
|---|---|---|
| The opening is a **barrier that parts**: velvet or pearl curtains, envelopes with wax seals, doors, shutters, scratch foil | The Digital Yes, invifest, mallikainvitestudio, our own legacy demos | The opening is **something being made**: light finds a card, a pearl arrives, a strand is strung. Nothing blocks the view; the invitation assembles in front of you. |
| Painted watercolour couples seen from behind | mallikainvitestudio, The Digital Yes | Photographed materials and moments, graded to one light |
| Script fonts for names | nearly everyone | Condensed high-contrast serif at masthead scale; no script anywhere |
| Pale from top to bottom | mallikainvitestudio | Dawn-to-night colour build |
| The same running order for every couple | mallikainvitestudio | Scene compositions tied to this design's concept |
| Gold as the luxury signal | invifest, red-gold templates | Nacre (pearl lustre) as the luxury signal; gold only as thread |

**Studied for quality, not copied:** luxury stationery (engraving and blind deboss on cotton paper), fashion
editorial layouts (masthead scale, asymmetric bleeds, captions), perfume and jewellery films (macro
materials, one moving object, controlled light), and Indian textile houses' still-life photography (craft,
hands, natural light, restraint).

## 4. The ten questions

### 4.1 What makes Mogra & Moti visually recognisable?

**The strand**: a single champagne silk thread carrying alternating pearls and closed mogra buds, lying
across ivory cotton paper in raking morning light. Around it: white-on-white materials told apart only by
texture and light (paper, pearl, petal, sheer chanderi), and two very tall, narrow names held apart by that
thread instead of joined by an ampersand.

*Why:* recognisability comes from one repeatable object plus one repeatable light, not from a pattern.
A still of any scene should be identifiable by the thread, the light direction and the white-on-white
palette.

### 4.2 What is the signature opening moment?

**The light finds their names.** In near-darkness, morning light rakes across a blind-debossed card and the
monogram rises out of the paper as relief — it was always there, the light reveals it. A single pearl rolls
in and stops as its thread pulls tight; the rest of the strand is drawn onto the card bead by bead; the names
are uncovered by the same light. Tapping the pearl pulls the thread: the strand slides away, a mogra bud
passes the camera, and the camera moves into the paper.

*Why:* it replaces the market's "barrier that parts" with cause and effect between real materials. Every
motion has a physical reason (light moves → relief appears; pearl stops → thread tightens → strand follows;
tap → pull → strand leaves). Full frame-by-frame in `animation-storyboard.md` §7.

### 4.3 What is the signature material?

**Nacre** — the layered lustre of pearl: a soft, travelling highlight with a blush and sage overtone, never
metallic.

*Why:* gold foil is the default luxury cue in Indian wedding design and reads as "template" on screen. Nacre
is ownable (it is literally *moti*), it matches a white-on-white palette, and its behaviour — light moving
slowly across a surface — is a motion idea as much as a colour. Nacre appears in highlights only: the pearl,
the music control, the reception scene, the final frame. Details in `material-library.md` §1.

### 4.4 What is the signature transition?

**The Draw.** A thread enters, tightens, and draws the next scene up over the previous one like a new sheet of
paper pulled into place; beads slide along the thread as it goes. Used three times only: into Celebrations,
into the RSVP, and into the Closing, where the draw ends in the knot.

*Why:* it turns the concept (stringing, joining) into the page's grammar, it reads as physical rather than
digital, and it is cheap to render (a translating sheet with a curved edge, not a full-screen mask). Three
uses keeps it an event, not a tic. Spec in `animation-storyboard.md` §3.

### 4.5 What is the signature typography treatment?

**Names held apart by the thread.** "Rohan" sits high and flush left, "Anaya" low and flush right, in Imbue
at masthead scale (about 130 px on a 390 px phone), with the thread running diagonally through the gap
between them where an "&" would be. Surnames sit beneath each name in tiny expanded capitals. It appears
exactly three times: the opening, the welcome, and the closing.

*Why:* the ampersand joining two names is the most templated element in wedding design. Replacing it with
the strand makes the union the concept's object, and the scale contrast (names about 9× the body size) is
what makes a phone screen feel like an editorial cover rather than a card. Spec in `typography-system.md` §4.

### 4.6 What is the signature image treatment?

**Graded to the hour, bleeding off one edge, settling into place.** Every photograph is colour-graded to its
chapter's light (cool dawn to lamp-warm night), shares the paper's grain, bleeds off exactly one screen edge
(never floats with equal margins; the few declared full-screen moments are listed in `visual-dna.md` §3), and
enters by a mask opening from that edge while the image inside settles from slightly large to rest.

*Why:* consistency of light makes photos from different sources feel like one shoot; one-edge bleed is an
editorial layout habit that breaks the centred-card template; the settle gives images weight without the
banned fade-up. Spec in `visual-dna.md` §3.

### 4.7 What makes it feel Indian without becoming "Indian wedding"?

Meaning through **materials, rituals of the hand and language**, not through ornament:

- mogra strung as buds that bloom at night (a true, lived detail, not a motif)
- pearls and their Hyderabadi association, the thread of the mangalsutra, the gathbandhan knot at the Wedding
- chikankari *shadow work* on sheer chanderi — ornament that exists only as a soft shadow, white on white
- mehendi's real stain colour as the one warm accent in the Mehendi scene, photographed, not drawn
- Devanagari numerals as chapter marks (०१ … ०८), set as typography
- santoor in the music: struck strings that sound like pearls dropping

Explicitly absent: mandalas, paisleys, elephants, Ganesha clip-art, red-and-gold, marigold swags, diyas as
icons, palace silhouettes, dhol graphics. A family's invocation line is supported as optional text, because
it is a ritual requirement for many families, not decoration.

*Why:* stereotype comes from borrowed symbols used as decoration. Specific materials and gestures carry
culture with more authenticity and far more restraint.

### 4.8 What makes it premium rather than template-based?

1. **Every scene has its own composition.** No repeated section recipe; four celebrations, four layouts.
2. **Real materials in one consistent light.** One key light from the top left across every scene, graded
   photography, shared grain.
3. **Scale contrast.** Masthead names against 11 px labels; nothing medium-sized for the sake of it.
4. **Restraint.** At most three elements compete in any viewport; ornament is a shadow, not a fill.
5. **Choreography with causes.** Nothing moves without a reason another element can see.
6. **Specific words.** Short, written copy per scene; no filler headings.
7. **No UI furniture.** No cards, pills, icons or gradients; links are text with a thread underline.
8. **Sound design** that is sparse and synced.
9. **Performance** that makes it feel expensive: a first screen that moves at 60 fps on a mid-range phone.

### 4.9 What is the visual hierarchy on a 390 px phone?

Per viewport, in this order, and never more than one of each:

1. **The moment** — the one image or the names (fills 45–70% of the viewport)
2. **The fact** — the event name or date (the only other large type)
3. **The action** — one text link or the one button, in the lower 40% of the screen (thumb zone)
4. **The detail** — time, venue, dress note, caption (small, grouped, left-aligned)

Chrome stays out of the way: the "Sample invitation" mark top left, the music pearl bottom right, both small.
Full grid and spacing in `visual-dna.md` §5.

### 4.10 What should the guest remember ten seconds after closing it?

**"The light found their names, a pearl rolled across the card, and by the end every flower had opened."**
The image they keep is the final frame: a knotted strand of open mogra and pearls under one warm lamp, with the
date. If they can almost smell the mogra, the invitation worked.

## 5. Design principles

Each is a decision rule, with the reason it exists.

| # | Principle | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Light before objects, objects before words.** Materials are revealed by light; words arrive last in each moment. | It makes the page feel physical and gives the eye a reason to wait. |
| 2 | **One lead motion per moment.** Other elements only react to it. | Cause and effect is what separates choreography from "things animating". |
| 3 | **White on white, told apart by texture.** Paper, pearl, petal and silk are all near-white. | Luxury through material rather than colour; it also makes the night scenes land. |
| 4 | **The thread is sacred.** It appears only where it joins something: names, scenes, the knot, a reply. | Overuse would turn the signature into a decoration. |
| 5 | **Asymmetry everywhere except the Wedding.** The pheras scene is the only centred, symmetrical composition. | Symmetry is reserved for the sacred moment so that it carries weight. |
| 6 | **Nothing is a card.** Information sits on paper, in grids, separated by space and hairlines. | Cards are the most templated object on the web. |
| 7 | **Every word earns its place.** No section title without a line of meaning; no line without a job. | Copy is part of the art direction (the teardown's best lesson). |
| 8 | **Reduced motion is a shorter cut, not a still.** | The user's instruction; also most phones with battery saver report it. |
| 9 | **If it wouldn't survive a screenshot, fix the composition, not the animation.** | Most people see invitations as forwarded screenshots. |

## 6. Anti-patterns

The master list. Scene-specific ones are in `animation-storyboard.md`. If any of these appears, it is a defect.

**Openings and structure**
- Curtains (velvet or pearl), envelopes, wax seals, doors, shutters, scratch foil as the opening
- A hero section: centred names, a date, a "scroll" cue
- Sections built as title → lede → bordered box, repeated
- Four identical event cards; a timeline rail with dots
- Countdown tiles
- A floating saturated "Get this design" pill over the artwork

**Imagery**
- Stock couples grinning at the camera; AI-generated faces; generic "bride looking over shoulder"
- Text inside images; watermarks; logos
- HDR, oversaturated reds, teal-and-orange grading, sparklers, drone shots of palaces
- Clip-art or vector flowers; drawn mehendi patterns; mandalas, paisleys, elephants, Ganesha illustrations
- Photos with equal margins on all sides floating in the middle of the screen

**Type and colour**
- Script or calligraphic fonts; "&" between the names
- Gold gradients as fills; red-and-gold; pure #FFFFFF or #000000
- Drop shadows or glows on type; outlined type
- Letter-spaced lowercase hint lines; more than three type sizes in one viewport

**Motion**
- Fade-up on scroll; scale-in; bounce; rotate-in; blur-in
- Particle systems: falling petals, confetti, sparkles, bokeh, hearts
- Continuous floating or bobbing objects; pulsing "tap to open" text
- Parallax on body text; typewriter or letter-by-letter random reveals
- Scroll-jacking beyond the pinned budget; horizontal scroll hijacked from vertical on phones
- Several elements starting at the same moment and moving independently

**Interface**
- Icons (map pins, calendar glyphs, hearts) where a word will do
- Pill buttons, gradient buttons, glassmorphism, drop-shadow cards
- Autoplay sound before a tap; sound that can't be muted
- Emoji, "❤️", exclamation marks, "Save the date!" clichés
